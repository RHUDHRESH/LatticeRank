## 2. Root cause: these are exact periodic lattice aliases

### 2.1 Method

A naive `dx / nominal_pitch` test is biased, because the Search capture applies its own
scale (0.992–1.008) and rotation (±0.35°). Over 20–52 lattice periods those accumulate
into several tenths of a period of apparent residual — enough to hide the effect.

`diagnostics/lattice_fit.py` instead builds the **actual lattice basis of the captured
Search image**:

```
a = s·(pitch_x/10)·( cos t, sin t)      b = s·(pitch_y/10)·(−sin t, cos t)
```

with `s`, `t` the true Search scale and rotation, then solves `(dx, dy) = m·a + n·b` for
real `(m, n)` and measures the distance to the nearest integer pair.

### 2.2 Result

`MEASURED RESULT` — **all 4 failures land within 0.50–2.12 px of an exact integer lattice
translate of the ground truth**, while being 61–614 px away from it.

The residual is of the same magnitude as the *median localisation error on correct pairs*
(0.52 px). In other words: **the locator is finding the pattern with its normal precision —
it is simply finding it in the wrong unit cell.** For the 16 correct pairs the same fit
returns `(m, n) = (0, 0)` with residuals 0.09–1.05 px, confirming the fit is well-posed.

Under a strict `|residual| ≤ 0.25` criterion in *lattice units*, 3 of 4 qualify
(validation-000006 misses at 0.257). Measured in **pixels** — the physically meaningful
unit — all 4 qualify overwhelmingly.

### 2.3 The parity confirmation

`MEASURED RESULT` — all four aliases have **`m + n` even**.

This is not a coincidence. DriftForge places contacts on a checkerboard
(`scene.py:142`: `parity = ((ix + iy) & 1) == 0`), exactly as the sponsor does
("one contact per 2 cells, matching real folded-bitline layouts"). A translation
`(m·pitch_x, n·pitch_y)` preserves the line grid for **any** integers, but preserves the
**contact sub-lattice only when `m+n` is even**. Odd translates misalign every contact and
score measurably worse.

Probability of all four being even by chance: (1/2)⁴ = 6.25%.

**Conclusion:** the failures are not numerical accidents, wrong-scale artifacts or noise
corruption. They are **true symmetries of the rendered pattern**. The locator selected a
position that is genuinely, physically almost-identical to the correct one.

`CODE OBSERVATION` — this also fixes the correct lattice for v2 candidate expansion. The
alias set is the **centred-rectangular lattice** generated by `(2·pitch_x, 0)` and
`(pitch_x, pitch_y)` — *not* the naive rectangular `(pitch_x, pitch_y)` grid. Expanding on
the wrong lattice would generate half the candidates as provably-worse odd translates and
waste the reranker's budget.

### 2.4 Failure classification (final)

| Class | Count | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| `periodic_alias` (integer lattice translate, parity-consistent) | **4 / 4** | §2.2, §2.3 |
| NMS candidate suppression (sole cause) | 0 | — but see §3, it is a *contributing* cause |
| wrong scale | 0 | selected scales 0.94–1.06 all within the generator's range |
| wrong rotation | 0 | selected rotations within ±3°, generator range ±2.55° |
| low-SNR corruption | 0 | all four have psr 4.0–6.1, above the 3.0 gate |
| boundary/context mismatch | 0 | 2 `standard`, 2 `ambiguous`; no `boundary` pair failed |
| generator shortcut | — | separate axis, see §4 — it is the reason the other 16 *pass* |
| transform-model mismatch | 0 | — |
| numerical/implementation bug | 0 | reproduces bit-identically |

The earlier heuristic label `remote_rival_low_gap` for validation-000016 is **superseded**:
the rigorous fit shows a 0.50 px residual on a (−15, −35) translate. It is a periodic
alias with an essentially zero peak gap (0.0005), which is precisely what an exact
wallpaper produces.

### 2.5 Did the abstention gate work?

`MEASURED RESULT` — yes. All four failures have `accepted = False`
(peak_gap 0.0005–0.0166, all below the 0.020 threshold), which is why
`accepted_incorrect@5px = 0`. Coverage 75% = 15/20 accepted (4 failures + 1 correct
low-gap pair rejected).

**But this is worth much less than it appears.** The contest requires a coordinate for
every pair. The gate correctly identifies *that* the system is lost; it does nothing to
find the right cell. On the emitted coordinate, DriftForge v1 scores 80% at 5 px, and the
20% it misses is exactly the pre-announced hard case.

The one genuinely valuable signal: `peak_gap` separates the two populations cleanly here
(failures 0.0005–0.0166, correct pairs 0.0178–0.3477). That makes it a strong **feature**
for a v2 meta-ranker — not a reason to keep abstention as the output policy.

## 3. H1 — NMS is destroying legitimate periodic candidates

`baseline.py:82`: `footprint = max(5, int(round(min(template_shape) * 0.30)))`.
Templates are 94–106 px, so the footprint is **28–32 px**. A peak survives only if it is
the maximum of its own N×N window, so two rivals closer than ~footprint/2 ≈ 15 px cannot
coexist.

`MEASURED RESULT` — lattice periods at Search resolution vs the NMS footprint:

| Preset | pitch_x (px) | pitch_y (px) | NMS/pitch_x | NMS/pitch_y | lattice sites per NMS cell |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `finfet_dense` (min pitch) | 2.80 | 4.80 | 10.7 | 6.3 | **67.0** |
| `dram_dense` (min pitch) | 4.80 | 7.20 | 6.3 | 4.2 | 26.0 |
| `finfet_open` (min pitch) | 5.00 | 7.50 | 6.0 | 4.0 | 24.0 |
| `finfet_dense` (max pitch) | 5.00 | 7.80 | 6.0 | 3.9 | 23.1 |
| `dram_open` (min pitch) | 7.50 | 10.00 | 4.0 | 3.0 | 12.0 |
| `dram_dense` (max pitch) | 7.20 | 11.00 | 4.2 | 2.7 | 11.4 |
| `finfet_open` (max pitch) | 9.00 | 13.00 | 3.3 | 2.3 | 7.7 |
| `dram_open` (max pitch) | 12.00 | 17.00 | 2.5 | 1.8 | **4.4** |

**Answer to the H1 question: yes, decisively.** In *every* preset, and across the whole
parameter range, a single NMS neighbourhood contains between **4.4 and 67 physically
distinct lattice hypotheses**. Only one survives, and which one survives is decided by the
noise realisation, not by the evidence.

Two consequences, both real:

1. **The official centre rule is corrupted at the source.** `baseline.py:165-166` picks the
   tied candidate nearest the Search centre — but from a list where each ~30 px
   neighbourhood is represented by one arbitrarily-chosen member. The nearest *true*
   equivalent match can be up to ~15 px from the surviving representative and may not be
   in the list at all. DriftForge cannot correctly implement the rule it claims to
   implement.
2. **Local error floor.** Even when the correct neighbourhood wins, the returned point is
   the luckiest peak in that cell, not the best one.

`CODE OBSERVATION` — H1 is **confirmed as a design defect**, but it is *not* the direct
cause of the 61–614 px jumps (those are remote, far beyond one NMS cell). NMS is a
**candidate-set integrity** problem; the aliasing is a **scoring/evidence** problem. Both
must be fixed, and fixing NMS alone would not have saved any of the four failures.

*(No production code was modified to establish this; the footprint arithmetic is read
directly from the shipped source and compared against the shipped presets.)*

## 4. H2 — the target-correlated signature is a severe shortcut. **This is the headline finding.**

### 4.1 What the code does

`scene.py:205-219`, executed for every profile with `defect_density > 0` (i.e. everything
except `ambiguous`):

```python
signature_rng = np.random.default_rng(spec.seed * 104_729 + 43)
sx = spec.target_cx_nm + signature_rng.uniform(-260, 260)
sy = spec.target_cy_nm + signature_rng.uniform(-260, 260)
radius = signature_rng.uniform(72, 135)
...  img[missing] *= U(0.08, 0.28)     # dark core
     img[rim]     = max(img, U(0.72, 0.90))   # bright rim
```

`CODE OBSERVATION`:
- The signature is placed at the **target centre ± 260 nm**, with radius ≤ 135 nm. Since
  `260 + 135 = 395 < 500`, it is **always fully inside the 1000 nm Reference FOV** —
  in 100% of non-`ambiguous` samples.
- Its radius (72–135 nm) is **larger than every random defect** (18–85 nm), and it is the
  only defect with a *dark-core-plus-bright-rim* morphology. The four random kinds are a
  dark patch, a bright blob, a bright bar and a bright scratch — none has a rim.
- At Search scale it is a **14–27 px** high-contrast blob — right in the pass-band of the
  σ=3 − σ=15 mid-band channel that carries **75%** of the fused score.
- Random defects number only `round(10 · density)` = 4–14 across the entire 10 µm world,
  so a distinctive blob of this size inside the Reference FOV is effectively a unique
  fingerprint.

In short: the generator plants a large, unique, high-contrast marker at the answer, and
the locator weights the exact frequency band that sees it most strongly.

### 4.2 Measurement

`diagnostics/ablation.py` removes **only** the signature block via a runtime monkeypatch
(random defects, geometry, noise and all seeds unchanged) and re-runs the shipped
selection logic. `ambiguous` pairs are unaffected by construction and were verified
byte-identical across conditions — a passing sanity check on the patch.

`MEASURED RESULT`, 20 validation pairs, fused channel:

| Condition | median err | P95 | max | acc ≤5 px | failures >25 px |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **With signature (as shipped)** | 0.60 px | 559.0 | 614.4 | **80%** | 4 |
| **Signature removed** | **273.01 px** | 752.2 | 895.9 | **30%** | **14** |

**Of the 16 pairs the shipped system gets right, 10 (62%) collapse to catastrophic
failure when the signature is removed.** Only 6 survive
(validation-000002/8/9/14/15/19 — note 4 of the 6 are `boundary` profile, i.e. they are
solved by genuine mat/periphery context rather than by the marker).

### 4.3 Verdict

`MEASURED RESULT` — **DriftForge v1's headline 80% accuracy is majority-explained by
information its own generator leaks about the answer, not by cross-scale matching skill.**

The hidden sponsor test data cannot contain this signature: the sponsor's only structural
defect is pattern collapse/bridging, applied uniformly to the whole canvas and
uncorrelated with the Reference crop location (`src/structural_defects.py`, verified —
there is no other defect model in the repository).

`HYPOTHESIS` (to be tested by E8, cross-generator) — DriftForge v1's true hidden-test
accuracy is closer to the **30–45%** regime than to 80%. The 80% figure should not be
quoted anywhere in the submission without this caveat.

This is a **Phase-1 stop-condition trigger** ("target-correlated generator leakage is found
to dominate performance") and is escalated in §7 and in `07_RISK_REGISTER.md` R2.
Nothing was deleted in Phase 1 — the classification is the deliverable.

## 5. H3 — transform search cost

`MEASURED RESULT` — 79.6% of the 3.69 s/pair runtime is the 50 `_ncc_valid` calls
(25 variants × 2 channels) at 57.8 ms each. Full table and budget arithmetic in
`04_BASELINE_REPRODUCTION.md` §5.

The bank is also **mis-specified for the hidden test**: it searches 5 rotations because
DriftForge's *own* generator produces up to ±2.55° of relative rotation. The sponsor
generator produces **exactly zero** rotation and zero scale variation. Every extra
transform variant is not merely wasted compute — it is an extra opportunity for a spurious
peak to win the global arg-max, since scores are compared across variants with different
NCC bias (`03` D4).

## 6. H4 — the fixed 0.25/0.75 fusion is tuned to the leak

`MEASURED RESULT`:

| Channel | With signature | Signature removed |
|---|---:|---:|
| raw NCC only | 75% @5 px | **45%** @5 px |
| mid-band NCC only | 80% | **15%** |
| fused 0.25 raw + 0.75 mid-band (shipped) | 80% | **30%** |
| *oracle* best-of-two | 80% | **45%** |

Two findings:

1. **With the signature present, fusion buys almost nothing** — it matches the mid-band
   channel alone (80% vs 80%), improving only the median (0.60 vs 0.91 px).
2. **With the signature removed, the fixed fusion is actively harmful.** Raw NCC alone
   (45%) beats the shipped fusion (30%) by 15 points, because the 0.75 weight on the
   mid-band is weighting exactly the band that carried the marker. On leak-free data the
   mid-band channel is the *worst* of the three (15%).

`MEASURED RESULT` — **expert disagreement rises from 4/20 to 14/20** when the signature is
removed, and the oracle best-of-two (45%) beats the fixed fusion (30%) by 15 points.
There is real, exploitable headroom for a candidate-level meta-ranker that *learns* when
to trust which channel, instead of a hard-coded weight pair.

`RECOMMENDATION` — do not simply re-tune the 0.25/0.75 weights. Any fixed weighting is a
single point on a curve that the data says should be sample-dependent. Keep both channels
as **features**, and let a ranker arbitrate (Expert G).
